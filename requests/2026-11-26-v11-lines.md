# v11 along three lines of shapes, on Grace and MareNostrum 5 (the third issue of the v11 request, 2026-11-26)

> **When to reply.** Send one reply, through the person who gave you this request, when the request is complete on both clusters (step 5). Message earlier only if something stops the request and you cannot repair it under "What you may change" below.

Read `README.md` in this directory first.

**This is a new request in the series of 2026-11-20, and it starts nothing over.** The earlier issue's results are in hand and its jobs are finished; leave its directory as it is. This one has its own bundle, its own directory and its own three plans. Launch nothing from earlier requests.

## What this is

The queue measured here is `ubq::v11` in the crate, version 11 of UBQ. The earlier issue measured it at nine shapes. This one measures it, beside five other queues, along three lines of shapes: X producers and one consumer, X producers and X consumers, and one producer and X consumers, with X doubling from 1 to the size of a socket, and the largest of each line across both sockets. At the largest shape of each line on one socket it also measures batch sizes, for the three queues that take batches: v11, BQ (a published batched FIFO) and a mutex over a deque. It also measures what each machine charges for one shared word, with a small benchmark of its own.

**It is one build job and an array of ten tasks per cluster.** The build job builds the binaries on a node, tests them, runs a smoke test and times GPFS once (17 minutes at home). Each task then has one node for at most 90 minutes and runs every cell of the three plans in an order of its own: two trials of each cell of `lines`, one of each cell of `correct` and of `price`. From the trial times of a full job on a two-socket node of 160 cores at home, a task takes about 40 minutes: about 7 node-hours per cluster, at most 17 at the limits. The tasks start when the build has succeeded, as many at once as the scheduler allows. Each is a whole measurement on one node, so the order the tasks run in, and their nodes, do not matter.

The earlier issues ran two jobs of under two hours, because creating files on GPFS was slow. A task now keeps every file on its node and creates on GPFS only its few result and log files, when it ends, so ten short jobs cost little more than two long ones, and the scheduler can fit them where it has room.

A task keeps everything on its node's own disk (`$TMPDIR`) and writes to GPFS once, when it ends; the one exception is the binaries the build job makes, which go to GPFS as soon as they are built, so that the tasks can take them. While a task runs, `$ROOT/results` and its directory under `$ROOT/logs` are therefore empty, and its Slurm log holds a few lines: that is expected. Only the build job times GPFS against the node's disk (`probe.tsv`).

## What you may change, and what you may not

The person who sent this cannot reach these machines, and you can. **If something in this request's machinery does not work here, repair it and go on; do not stop and wait.**

You may change:
- `kit/job.sh` and `kit/drive.py`: where files are kept, which directory is the node's own disk, module names, the memory-policy wrapper, time limits, anything about starting and collecting;
- the `sbatch` options of step 2 (account, partition, QOS, time, constraint, and a cap on the tasks that run at once, such as `--array=1-10%4`), if the scheduler refuses them or another choice starts sooner;
- in `kit/make_plans.py`, only what is needed for it to read the node's cores.

You may not change:
- the source and vendor trees inside the bundle, or the binaries: they are always built from the bundle by `job.sh`;
- what is measured: in `kit/make_plans.py` the queues, shapes, batches, delays, policies, warm-up, durations and numbers of trials;
- a result file's contents.

A trial that crashes, hangs, is killed at its limit or miscounts is a **result**. It is recorded and reported, not repaired. The limit is 180 s a trial. A cell two of whose trials were killed at it is not run again in that task: its other trials are recorded as `not run`, so a result file still has one line for every trial of its plan. The same holds if v11's unit tests fail: report it in the reply and go on.

Before you change a kit file, copy it to `$ROOT/kit/orig/`. The reply carries `diff -u` of every changed file against its original, with a sentence saying why. A job already in the queue runs the `job.sh` it was submitted with: after a repair to `job.sh`, cancel the queued tasks and submit them again.

## 2. The build job and the array, submitted together

The jobs use the account, partition and QOS that the earlier issue's jobs used on the same cluster. This shows them:

```sh
sacct -X -u $USER -S 2026-11-20 -E 2026-11-24 -o jobid,jobname%16,account%14,partition%12,qos%14,elapsed,state -P | grep -E 'JobID|v11-'
```

Set `ACCOUNT`, `P` (the partition) and `Q` (the QOS the measuring jobs ran in) from it on each cluster, and `QA` to that cluster's debug QOS, if your account has one (`sacctmgr show assoc user=$USER format=account,partition,qos -P` lists what it may use). Then, on the Grace login node, and on the MareNostrum 5 login node with the second line's values:

```sh
export ROOT=$PROJ/ubq-v11-lines
: ${ACCOUNT:?set ACCOUNT} ${P:?set P} ${Q:?set Q} ${QA:=$Q}
C=grace; N=144; M=--mem=0     # on MareNostrum 5: C=mn5; N=112; M=
mkdir -p $ROOT/logs/$C
S="--account=$ACCOUNT --partition=$P --nodes=1 --ntasks=1 --exclusive --cpus-per-task=$N --hint=nomultithread $M"
B=$(sbatch --parsable $S --qos=$QA --time=01:30:00 --job-name=v11L-build --output=$ROOT/logs/$C/build-%j.out $ROOT/kit/job.sh build $C $ROOT)
sbatch $S --qos=$Q --time=01:30:00 --array=1-10 --dependency=afterok:${B%%;*} --job-name=v11L-run --output=$ROOT/logs/$C/run-r%a-%A_%a.out $ROOT/kit/job.sh run $C $ROOT
squeue -u $USER -h -o '%i %j %T %M %r %N'
```

The ten tasks, `r1` to `r10`, are the same measurement in ten orders, on whatever nodes the scheduler gives them; they wait for the build job only, not for one another. If the build job fails, they never start (`squeue` shows them pending with `DependencyNeverSatisfied`): step 3 says what to do.

**The build job asks for the debug QOS**, because it is short and tells whether the machinery works here. `sacctmgr show qos $QA format=name,maxwall,maxtrespu,maxjobspu,maxsubmitpu -P` shows its limits; if it does not take a job of 90 minutes with a node to itself, submit the build with `$Q`. If `$Q` takes fewer than ten jobs from one user (`maxsubmitpu`), submit the array in parts that it takes (`--array=1-5`, and later `--array=6-10` without `--dependency`). A limit on the jobs that run at once (`maxjobspu`) needs nothing: the scheduler holds the rest until there is room.

What the build job and each task do, on their node's disk:
1. **The binaries.** The build job unpacks the bundle, builds the runner and the line benchmark offline with the `gcc` and `rust` modules (`cargo build --offline --locked --release`, the flags of the earlier issue), runs v11's unit tests, stores both binaries under `$ROOT/builds/$C/` and runs the **smoke test**: one short trial of a few cells of every plan. It then times GPFS against the node's disk. Each task copies the binaries that are there; a task that finds none (one submitted without the build) builds them itself, or waits for a build that is going on, thirty minutes at most. Every trial records the hash of the binary it ran. If the line benchmark alone fails to build, the plan `price` is empty and the tasks go on: say so in the reply, with `$ROOT/logs/$C/build-<job>/build-linebench.log`.
2. **The three plans, in order**, each to its own result file on the node, stored as `$ROOT/results/$C/<plan>-r<task>.jsonl` when the task ends:

| plan | the question | what it runs | trials per task (Grace / MareNostrum 5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `correct` | do v11 and BQ deliver every element exactly once here? | v11 and BQ, single elements and batches of 16, 256 and 4,096, at the three shapes where `lines` measures batches and at the three across both sockets; a mutex over a deque as a control | 60 / 60 |
| `lines` | how does each queue's throughput go along the three lines? | X producers and one consumer, X a side, one producer and X consumers, X = 1, 2, 4, ... up to a socket, on one socket with memory bound to it, and the largest of each line across both sockets with memory interleaved. Single elements: v11, RBBQ, SegQueue, LSCQ, BQ and a mutex over a deque, at every shape. Batches of 4 to 4,096: v11, BQ and the mutex at the largest shape of each line on one socket. With no delay and with 100 ns of work on each side of every element | 792 / 720 |
| `price` | what does this machine charge for one shared word? | `linebench3`: w threads advance one word by a fetch-and-add, a swap from the value last seen, a load then a swap, or a load then a swap with a spin before each retry, with 0, 25 or 100 ns of work between successes; and one thread adding to the word while r threads load it. 300 ms a trial | 148 / 148 |

A shape such as `16p16c` is sixteen producers and sixteen consumers; the shapes are computed on the node from its cores. Where a queue passes few elements a second (LSCQ and the mutex with one producer and 32 or more consumers on single elements; BQ on single elements across both sockets), its rounds are shorter (4,096 elements), so that its trials end within a minute or so: some of them may still be killed at 180 s, which is a result. `price` pins its threads to CPUs 0, 1, 2, ... in order; if a task's CPUs are not numbered one per core from 0 the plan is empty, and `plans.txt` in the task's log directory says why: report it.

Over the ten tasks each cell of `lines` has twenty trials and each cell of `correct` and `price` ten.

## 3. Wait, and read each job's last line

**Wait for them yourself.** Every ten minutes or so, run `squeue -u $USER -h -o '%i %j %T %M %r %N'` until none of the `v11L-` jobs is left. Then:

```sh
export ROOT=$PROJ/ubq-v11-lines
C=grace   # on MareNostrum 5: C=mn5
tail -qn 1 $ROOT/logs/$C/build-*.out $ROOT/logs/$C/run-*.out
grep -h -E 'BUILD DONE|BUILD FAILED|SMOKE FAILED|smoke test:|unit tests ended|line benchmark' $ROOT/logs/$C/build-*.out
```

- `RUN DONE`: nothing more for that task.
- `RUN INCOMPLETE`: its time ended first, or Slurm ended it at its limit, or it was cancelled; it stored what it had measured before it ended. Submit that task again: the array's line of step 2 with `--array=<its number>` (the `r<number>` in its log's name; several as `--array=3,7`) and without `--dependency`. It copies the binaries, starts from its result files and runs only what is missing. Up to three times per task.
- `BUILD FAILED`: the tasks never start; cancel them (`scancel <the array's job id>`). The last forty lines of the build are in the build job's log, the whole in `$ROOT/logs/$C/build-<job>/build.log`. Repair what belongs to the machinery (a module name, a directory) and submit step 2 again. If the source itself does not compile on this machine, stop for this cluster and say so in the reply, with the log.
- `SMOKE FAILED`: every cell of the smoke test failed, so the machinery is at fault, and the tasks never start; cancel them. `$ROOT/logs/$C/build-<job>/smoke.txt` says what each cell said. Repair and submit step 2 again.
- `BUILD DONE` with a unit-test status other than 0, or a smoke test with some failing cells: these are results. The tasks go on to the plans; report them.
- `RUN FAILED`, or a log that ends in anything else: the machinery. Read the log and the task's directory under `$ROOT/logs/$C/`, repair, and submit that task again.
- A task's log that stops in the middle, with no directory for it under `$ROOT/logs/$C/` and nothing new under `$ROOT/results/$C/`: the task died with no warning, and what it had measured was on the node. Submit that task again, and say so in the reply (`sacct -j <job> --format=jobid,state,exitcode,elapsed,nodelist` says how it ended).

## 4. Read the results

On each cluster's login node:

```sh
export ROOT=$PROJ/ubq-v11-lines
C=grace   # on MareNostrum 5: C=mn5
mkdir -p ~/v11-lines-2026-11-26
python3 -I $ROOT/kit/summarize.py $ROOT/results/$C/*.jsonl > ~/v11-lines-2026-11-26/summary-$C.txt
python3 -I $ROOT/kit/summarize.py --probes $ROOT/logs/$C/*/probe.tsv > ~/v11-lines-2026-11-26/probes-$C.txt
python3 - $ROOT/results/$C/*.jsonl <<'PY'
import json, sys
for path in sys.argv[1:]:
    lines = [json.loads(l) for l in open(path) if l.strip()]
    bad = sum(1 for r in lines if r.get("error") or not r.get("result"))
    print(path.split("/")[-1], len(lines), "of", lines[0]["of"] if lines else "?", "trials,", bad, "failed")
PY
```

A cluster is complete when each of its thirty result files (three plans, ten tasks) holds all of its trials. Trials that failed count as recorded: `summarize.py` lists them.
